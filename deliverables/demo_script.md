# Demo video script

Video: `demo_video.mp4` (recorded automatically by `scripts/record_demo.py`; presenter: Meet Kumar Jain)

| Time | Scene & narration |
|---|---|
| 0:00 | **Introduction** - Welcome! This is the capstone demo of our A.I.-powered PDF chatbot: a retrieval-augmented assistant inspired by the Adobe Acrobat A.I. Assistant. It lets you chat with a library of seventy-one PDF documents, or with any PDF you upload, and every answer is grounded in the documents, with clickable page citations. Everything runs locally on a laptop, so documents never leave the machine. |
| 0:26 | **Workflow** - The workflow follows the classic RAG pattern. A PDF is loaded and its text extracted, split into overlapping chunks, embedded into vectors, and stored in a vector database. At question time, the most relevant chunks are retrieved, combined with the question into a grounded prompt, and a large language model generates the answer, which is displayed in the chat interface. |
| 0:50 | **Architecture** - Here is the architecture in more detail. Offline, PDFs are loaded with PyPDF Loader and split by a recursive character splitter into chunks of one thousand characters with one hundred and fifty characters of overlap. Chunks are embedded with the G.T.E. large sentence-embedding model and stored in a FAISS index, next to a B.M. twenty-five keyword index. Online, an intent router separates questions from whole-document actions. Follow-up questions are condensed using the chat history. A hybrid retriever fuses vector and keyword results with reciprocal rank fusion, and a cross-encoder re-ranks them. If relevance is low, a self-correction step rewrites the query and retries. Finally, Gemma 3, a twelve-billion-parameter model served locally by LM Studio, writes the answer with citations. |
| 1:39 | **User interface** - This is the application. Just like Acrobat, the PDF viewer is on the left, with document selection, page navigation and zoom. The library contains company policies and product manuals, Adobe, Google and Microsoft annual reports, A.I. research papers, and NIST frameworks: more than two thousand three hundred pages in total. On the right is the Ask A.I. Assistant panel, with the familiar suggestion cards. |
| 2:06 | **One-click summary** - Let's open the Employee Leave Policy, and click Summarize. For a short document like this, the assistant reads the entire text; for long documents, it gathers representative passages with several focused searches. The answer streams in token by token. Notice the small numbered badges: every statement is linked to the passage it came from. |
| 2:28 | **Citations** - Clicking a source chip jumps the viewer to the cited page and highlights the exact passage in yellow, so the reader can verify the answer in seconds. This traceability is the key difference from a generic chatbot. |
| 2:41 | **Question answering** - Now a specific question: how long is parental leave for non-birthing parents? The hybrid retriever and re-ranker find the right paragraph, and the model answers precisely, with a citation. |
| 2:56 | **Conversation memory** - And a follow-up that only makes sense in context: can it be split into parts? Before searching, the assistant uses the chat history to rewrite this into a standalone question, so it knows that 'it' means parental leave for non-birthing parents. |
| 3:11 | **Hallucination guard** - What happens when the answer is not in the document? I'll switch on the retrieval trace, and ask about Apple's revenue. The re-ranker reports very low relevance, so the self-correction step rewrites the query and searches again. Still nothing relevant, so instead of hallucinating, the assistant clearly says it couldn't find the information. The trace shows every step: the standalone question, the scores, and the rewritten query. |
| 3:38 | **Long annual report** - Long documents work just as well. This is Adobe's fiscal 2024 Form 10-K, more than one hundred pages. I'll ask for total revenue and the performance of the Digital Media segment. The answer combines figures from different sections of the report, each with its page number. |
| 4:08 | **Search across all PDFs** - Switching the scope to All documents turns the assistant into a research tool across the whole library, like an Acrobat PDF Space. Here it compares revenue across two different annual reports, and the citation chips show which report each figure came from. |
| 4:29 | **Research papers** - The same works for research papers. Here is the original retrieval-augmented generation paper from twenty twenty. Identify what stands out picks out the key contributions and results, and as always, each point is cited. |
| 5:12 | **Compare models** - The language model is pluggable. A model picker lists every model served by LM Studio, and the Compare models switch answers the same question with several models side by side, using exactly the same retrieved passages. Here Gemma 3 twelve B, Llama 3.2 three B and Qwen 2.5 seven B answer a question about the support SLA. You can compare correctness, citations, length and latency at a glance: Qwen is the fastest, while the small Llama model is noticeably less precise. |
| 5:45 | **Upload your own PDF** - Users can also bring their own documents. I'll upload the capstone case study P.D.F. itself. It is extracted, chunked, embedded and indexed in a few seconds, and immediately becomes available for questions. Let's ask what deliverables the participants must submit. |
| 6:06 | **Evaluation** - To justify our design choices, we evaluated the system on thirty-three ground-truth questions. Over the whole library, dense vector search alone ranks the correct document first 88% of the time. Adding B.M. twenty-five and cross-encoder re-ranking raises this to 94%. End to end, 100% of answers contain the correct fact, 100% include citations, and 100% of the out-of-scope questions are correctly refused. Comparing local models with the same pipeline: Gemma 3 twelve B is 100% accurate in 4.6 seconds; Qwen 2.5 seven B is 100% accurate in 2.0 seconds; Llama 3.2 three B is 94% accurate in 2.5 seconds. |
| 6:53 | **Closing** - To summarize: a fully local and private PDF chatbot, built with LangChain, FAISS, sentence transformers, Streamlit and Gemma 3, with an Acrobat-style interface, hybrid retrieval, re-ranking, conversation memory, self-correction and verifiable citations. Thank you for watching! |
